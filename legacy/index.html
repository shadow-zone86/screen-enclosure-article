How Much Does a Screen Enclosure Cost in Florida?

Updated October 2026 4 min read Serving Southwest Florida

If you live in Florida, you already know that a screened outdoor space is less a luxury and more a way to enjoy your yard without the mosquitoes. The screen enclosure cost Florida homeowners budget for usually starts at $9,500 and can pass $33,500, depending on size and style. Below is a breakdown of the numbers, what moves them, and how long the work takes.

Three project types cover most of what gets built. Each starting price below is for standard finishes, and the final screen enclosure cost Florida homeowners pay depends on measurements, roof shape, and the options they choose.

What Moves the Screen Enclosure Cost in Florida

Two homes on one street can get quotes that differ by thousands of dollars. The usual reasons:

  • Size and roof shape — flat, gable, mansard, and multi-plane roofs use different amounts of aluminum and different anchoring
  • Mesh type — standard insect screen, no-see-um, or solar and privacy screens
  • Add-ons — LED lighting, ceiling fans, or extra concrete footers for the posts
  • Site access — how much decking or concrete has to be cut and restored

A pool cage costs more than a patio screen because it spans a larger opening and carries a full roof.

Permits, Engineering, and Wind Load

A screen enclosure is a permanent structure, so every Florida county requires a permit and an inspection. Plans must satisfy the Florida Building Code and local wind-load rules. In many areas the frame is engineered for 130+ mph winds, following what the local code and the approved drawings call for.

Engineering drawings usually take 1–2 weeks. Permit review runs from about one week in some counties to six to eight weeks in others. Once the permit is issued and materials are on site, installation takes 2–4 days.

Warranty and What Is Included

Coverage is up to 10 years on the aluminum structure, 5 years on the screen, and 365 days on the workmanship. Design, engineering, permit filing, and HOA approval can be handled for you, which removes the most tiring part of the project: the paperwork. A written quote should break out every line item, so the screen enclosure cost Florida homeowners agree to at the start is the price they pay at the end.
